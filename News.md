Here's a solid set of places to follow, grouped by what you're after. If you're doing ML projects like the one we just discussed, the first two categories will matter most.

**Research papers (the source of truth)**
- **arXiv** (arxiv.org, cs.LG and cs.AI sections) — where nearly all ML papers appear first, often before publication
- **Papers with Code** (paperswithcode.com) — papers linked to their code and leaderboards; great for finding state-of-the-art on a specific task
- **Google Scholar** — set up alerts for topics or authors you care about
- **Semantic Scholar** — good for tracing citations and related work

**Curated / digestible summaries** (so you don't drown in raw arXiv)
- **Hugging Face** (huggingface.co) — the daily papers page, plus the model and dataset hubs where new open models get released first
- **The Batch** by DeepLearning.AI — Andrew Ng's weekly newsletter, beginner-friendly
- **Import AI** (Jack Clark) and **Ahead of AI** (Sebastian Raschka) — thoughtful weekly newsletters
- **Papers with Code newsletter** and **arXiv Sanity** — help filter the firehose

**News and breakthroughs**
- **Company blogs** — this is where major model releases actually get announced: Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind, Meta AI, Mistral. Following these directly beats secondhand coverage.
- **TechCrunch AI**, **VentureBeat AI**, **MIT Technology Review** — general tech/AI journalism
- **Ars Technica** — good technical depth

**Community / discussion**
- **Reddit** — r/MachineLearning (research-focused), r/LocalLLaMA (open models)
- **Hacker News** (news.ycombinator.com) — breakthroughs surface here fast with critical discussion
- **X/Twitter** — many researchers post findings here first; follow authors of papers you like

**Courses / deeper learning**
- **DeepLearning.AI**, **Fast.ai**, and **Stanford CS231n / CS224n** (free lectures online) if you want structured foundations alongside the news

A practical routine that works for most people: skim **Hugging Face daily papers** or **The Batch** for the filtered version, follow **2–3 company blogs** directly for major releases, and dive into **arXiv + Papers with Code** only when something specific catches your interest. Trying to read everything leads to burnout fast.
